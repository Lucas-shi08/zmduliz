Notes on how
a product gets documented.

What a power rating actually measures, why one platform is published in two configurations, and why some data sheets carry a section with no curve in it. Written from the plant in Foshan.

Editorial rule Nothing published here claims a measurement we cannot send you the file for. Ask for the file and it comes with the quotation.
NOTE 01Platform
ZM-M65K

Two configurations, one basket

The ZM-M65K is published as two builds. ZM-M65K Comp is a 6.5″ two-way: a pair of 165 mm woofers, a pair of tweeters, a two-way crossover. ZM-M65K 3W Comp is the 6.5″ three-way: the same woofer pair, plus a midrange pair and a three-way crossover. Both use the same yellow-Kevlar cone, the same 90 mm ferrite motor and the same 25 mm silk-diaphragm tweeter on a neodymium magnet.

What changes is the middle of the band and the network that divides it. Adding a dedicated midrange moves the handover points, so the three-way is rated higher: 120 W AES / 240 W program against 90 W / 180 W for the two-way. Nominal impedance stays 4 Ω and sensitivity stays 90 dB on both.

That is the whole point of publishing them together. One tooling position, two positions in a line, and no surprise when two sheets with the same prefix carry different power figures.

NOTE 02Reading
a spec

AES power and program power are two different tests

Every power figure on our sheets carries the rating it was measured to. AES power is the continuous rating under a defined noise signal over a defined time. Continuous program power is the higher, music-like figure used across the aftermarket — on the M65K, 180 W against 90 W AES.

Neither number is “the” power of the loudspeaker. This is where comparison usually goes wrong: a platform rated 90 W AES is not weaker than one advertised at 120 W peak, even though 120 is the larger number on the page. Put the same rating side by side, or ask which test produced it.

We print both because most catalogues print one, and the one they print is usually the more flattering of the two. If a figure matters to your listing, ask us for the condition behind it before you print it on a box.

NOTE 03Data
policy

Why the M65K sheet has no frequency-response curve

Open the ZM-M65K data sheet and you will find a section on frequency response and impedance with no curve drawn in it. That is deliberate. The measurement files we hold are not fully consistent between the two configurations, and a curve that is correct for the two-way would be wrong for the three-way.

So the sheet says what it can support, and the curve is issued against the build you actually order — measured on the approved sample, on-axis and at 30° off-axis, together with the impedance file. The same rule applies to frame drawings where the source files disagree.

If a supplier sends a generic curve stapled to a catalogue sheet, it is fair to ask which build it was measured on. We would rather be asked that than be caught by it.
